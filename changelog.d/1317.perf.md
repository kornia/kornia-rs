`RgbdPyramid::rebuild_from_depth_mm` rebuilds an RGB-D pyramid in place from a new millimetre depth frame. It reuses the pyramid's vertex and normal maps and keeps the halved depths in a `PyramidScratch`, so a frame loop stops allocating once it has seen an image size. The result is bit-identical to `RgbdPyramid::from_depth_mm`. On a 320x200 frame with 3 levels, a warmed rebuild makes 0 allocations, against 17 for `from_depth_mm`. Its wall time drops from 5.5 to 4.6 ms, best of 9 rounds of 50 calls in a release build on an i7-9750H at load average 5 to 7, measured with a temporary `Instant` loop (no criterion bench yet).

`kornia_3d::registration::icp_projective_plane_with` takes an `IcpWorkspace` reused across calls, so a warmed ICP solve allocates nothing; it returns the same result as `icp_projective_plane`, bit for bit. The workspace is sized by the image, not by the inlier count, so a full frame after a sparse one does not reallocate. On a 320x200 three-level frame (corner and sphere scene, default criteria) the solve goes from 1 allocation to 0, and a frame of pyramid rebuild plus solve with `RgbdPyramid::rebuild_from_depth_mm` from 18 to 0. Wall time, best of 9 rounds of 50 calls in a release build on an i7-9750H at load average 5 to 7, measured with a temporary `Instant` loop: solve 28.0 to 27.7 ms (within noise), pyramid plus solve 33.7 to 31.9 ms.
